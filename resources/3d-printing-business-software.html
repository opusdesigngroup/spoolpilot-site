Best software for running a small 3D printing business

There isn't a best one. There is a right one for the question you are trying to answer and the number of orders you sell each month, and most small shops end up with two tools, not one. This page is a way to decide: five kinds of software, what each is for, a short set of questions that points at one, and where SpoolPilot fits, including where it doesn't.

By Sean, who runs Opus Design Company on Etsy: five years, 3,000+ sales. Last revised 12 September 2026.

Start from the question, not the tool

Four questions sort almost every shop.

  • Do you sell what you print? If not, you need at most a filament tracker, and possibly nothing.
  • How many orders do you sell a month? Under about twenty, a spreadsheet holds. Past that, copying each order's fee lines is the chore that breaks it.
  • Is the bottleneck money or machines? Not knowing what a product earns is a money problem. Not knowing which printer is free is a machine problem. Different software.
  • Do you sell on Etsy? If yes, the fees actually charged live in Etsy's payment ledger. A tool that reads that ledger directly is worth more than one that estimates fees from Etsy's published rates, and most do the latter, whatever the landing page says.

The five kinds of tool

A spreadsheet

Free, flexible, and the right starting point. It prices products well and produces a monthly total. It fails at real per-order fees (manual CSV copying), at variants (rows multiply), and at being where you are (the laptop, not the printer). It is sufficient until the copying takes longer than the printing.

A filament inventory tool

Spoolman and the many mobile apps like it track grams remaining per spool, often to the gram, with drying logs and printer integration. They have no concept of a sale, so they cannot tell you weeks of stock or what a product earned. Right when material precision is your actual problem. Tracking filament for a business explains why weight alone stops short, and Spoolman vs SpoolPilot compares directly.

Etsy analytics

Etsy's own Stats and payment account show views, orders, revenue, and the exact fee lines on every order. Third-party Etsy tools add search and listing analytics on top. None of them know your cost of goods, so none can show profit. Always worth reading; never the whole answer for a maker.

A pricing calculator

3DPCC, the free calculator on this site, and a dozen web tools turn grams, hours, and rates into a price before you list. They are for the moment before the sale. Most have no shop connection and no view of what an order actually netted afterward. How to price your prints is the method behind them; 3DPCC vs SpoolPilot compares one directly.

All-in-one 3D print business management

Software that connects the cost of a print to the sale it became: pricing, order sync, real fees, profit per product, filament counts, and what to print next in one place. SpoolPilot is one of these, built for iPhone and Etsy. Generic handmade bookkeeping such as Stocksmith (formerly Craftybase) is another shape of the same idea, desktop-first and not 3D-aware; the Craftybase comparison covers when it is the better choice. Print-farm managers (Bambu's farm tools, SimplyPrint) are sometimes lumped in here, but they manage machines and queues, not money, and they pair well with any of the above rather than replacing them.

Combinations that work

Spoolman for gram-level material detail plus something that knows your sales for the money. A farm manager for the printers plus anything from this list for profit. Etsy's own stats for traffic plus a tool that reads the ledger for fees. The mistake is expecting one product to do all of it; the second mistake is paying for two that answer the same question.

Where SpoolPilot fits

SpoolPilot is the all-in-one shape, deliberately narrow. It prices a print by reading the slicer screen with the iPhone camera (Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, Cura); syncs an Etsy shop read-only and reads the fees Etsy actually charged on each order directly from the payment ledger, not an estimate from the rate card; shows profit per order, per product, and per month; counts sealed spools by barcode with weeks of stock from sales pace; and ranks what to print next. Sales outside Etsy are logged by hand. Pricing, filament inventory, manual sales, and the cost model are free; real fees, the P&L, forecasts, and unlimited sync are Pro at $12.99 a month or $99.99 a year.

Reading a shop's payment ledger through the Etsy API requires Etsy's approval, which Etsy grants after reviewing what an app does and how it handles a seller's data. SpoolPilot went through that review and holds commercial API access. That is the difference between reading the fee lines Etsy posted for an order and estimating them from the published rate card, which is what most tools that promise "real profit" actually do. (The approval covers API access; Etsy does not endorse or certify the app.)

It is the wrong pick if you need printer control, tax or VAT handling, an Android or web version, sync with a platform other than Etsy, or quote tracking with client PDFs.
