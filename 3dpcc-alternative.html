3DPCC vs SpoolPilot: estimated fees, or the ones Etsy actually charged

3DPCC is a well-built cost calculator for 3D printing, and it does something SpoolPilot doesn't: it prices and quotes a job before you print it. But its Etsy feature estimates fees from the published rate card. SpoolPilot connects to your shop and reads what Etsy actually took. Here's the honest comparison.

The difference in one sentence

3DPCC answers "what should I charge for this print?" SpoolPilot answers "what did that order actually make me?" Those sound like the same question and they are not. One happens before the sale, from numbers you type. The other happens after, from your shop's real records.

Why estimated fees drift

Etsy publishes a rate card — transaction 6.5%, processing 3% + $0.25, listing $0.20, Offsite Ads 15% — and any calculator can apply it. The trouble is that what Etsy deposits often isn't that number. Offsite Ads is attributed partially rather than landing cleanly on the order you'd expect. Processing rates differ by country. Coupons change the base the percentage applies to. Regulatory fees appear in some regions and in no rate-card formula.

None of that makes a calculator wrong for pricing. It makes it the wrong tool for bookkeeping. If you want to know what your shop earned last month, the formula isn't good enough — you need the fee entries on the orders themselves.

Where 3DPCC is the better buy

Fair is fair, and this is a genuinely good app built by one developer. Pick 3DPCC if you mostly need to quote custom jobs before printing them — its draft-to-delivered quote tracking has no equivalent in SpoolPilot. Pick it if you need VAT, which SpoolPilot doesn't handle yet. Pick it if you want a one-time purchase rather than a subscription; $49.99 once is a real advantage over any monthly plan. And pick it if you don't sell on Etsy, because SpoolPilot's whole edge is reading a ledger you wouldn't have.

SpoolPilot costs more, and it should only win if you actually want the thing it does: your real sales, your real fees, and a profit number you can stand behind at the end of the month.
